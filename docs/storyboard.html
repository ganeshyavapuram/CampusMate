Scene 1

Getting started

Priya's first five minutes: signing in, setting up and staying in control of her account.

US-001

Sign in with Google

Not reviewed
Priya's moment

Priya opens CampusMate on the family laptop, taps "Sign in with Google" and lands on her dashboard. No new password to invent or forget.

Think of it like

Like walking into the college library with the ID card you already have, instead of applying for a separate library pass.

Why it's needed

Students already manage accounts for portals, email and many apps, and a separate CampusMate password would add friction plus a reset flow to build and secure. Google sign-in gets a student to their dashboard in one step and gives the back end a verified identity for every request.

As a student, I want to sign in with my Google account, so that I can use CampusMate without creating another password.

How we'll know it works (5 checks)
  • The login screen shows a "Sign in with Google" button and a one-paragraph introduction to CampusMate.
  • After a successful Google sign-in the student is taken to the dashboard (or to first-time setup, US-004, if they have no subjects).
  • On a student's first sign-in exactly one User record and one GamificationRecord are created; signing in again creates no new records.
  • A request to any protected API endpoint without a valid Firebase ID token returns HTTP 401 and no data (TC-01).
  • If the student closes the Google pop-up without signing in, they remain on the login screen and no error page is shown.

Not included: Email/password sign-in, other identity providers (Microsoft, GitHub, Apple), and any multi-factor authentication beyond what Google applies to the account.

Related decisions: ADR-004 ADR-011

US-002

Sign out

Not reviewed
Priya's moment

Priya finishes revising on a study-centre computer and taps "Sign out". The next student who sits down sees only the login screen.

Think of it like

Like locking your locker before you leave the library.

Why it's needed

Distance-learning students often use shared family computers or study-centre machines. Without a visible sign-out, the next person at that browser can see the student's notes, attendance and plans.

As a student, I want to sign out of CampusMate, so that my data is safe when I use a shared or public computer.

How we'll know it works (4 checks)
  • A "Sign out" control is reachable from every screen after sign-in (header or profile menu).
  • Selecting it returns the student to the login screen.
  • After signing out, pressing the browser Back button does not display any previously loaded student data.
  • After signing out, the front end holds no Firebase ID token, and the next API call from that browser is rejected with HTTP 401 until the student signs in again.

Not included: Signing out of all devices at once, and revoking Firebase tokens server-side before they expire.

Related decisions: ADR-004

US-003

Edit profile and delete account

Not reviewed
Priya's moment

Priya changes "Priya S" to "Priya Sharma". After graduating, she deletes her account and every note, plan and mark is gone.

Think of it like

Like handing back your hostel room key: you change the name on the door while you live there, and you clear everything out when you leave.

Why it's needed

The display name comes from Google and may not be what the student wants shown. A student who stops using the app has no way today to remove their notes, attendance and plans, which is a privacy gap once notes have been uploaded.

As a student, I want to change my display name and to delete my account with all its data, so that I control how I appear and what CampusMate keeps about me.

How we'll know it works (4 checks)
  • The profile screen lets the student change their display name; names of 1 to 100 characters are saved, and an empty name is rejected with a message.
  • "Delete account" opens a confirmation dialog stating that all subjects, assignments, study sessions, notes, quiz attempts, attendance and rewards will be permanently removed.
  • After confirmation, no rows belonging to that user remain in any table, any stored note files are removed, and the student is signed out.
  • Signing in again with the same Google account afterwards starts as a brand-new user with no data.

Not included: Exporting data before deletion, an undo or grace period, and deleting the Google account itself.

Open question

Should deletion wait for a grace period (for example 7 days) so an accidental deletion can be reversed?

US-004

Complete first-time setup

Not reviewed
Priya's moment

On her very first visit, CampusMate asks for her five subjects and how many hours she can study each day, so her dashboard isn't an empty page.

Think of it like

Like the first page of a new diary, where you write your timetable before anything else.

Why it's needed

Every CampusMate feature hangs off a subject, so a new student who lands on an empty dashboard has nothing to act on and may leave. A guided setup gets the minimum data in place in under two minutes.

As a student, I want a short setup on my first visit that asks for my subjects and daily study hours, so that the app is useful on day one instead of showing empty screens.

How we'll know it works (5 checks)
  • A signed-in student with zero subjects is shown the setup screen instead of the dashboard.
  • Setup collects at least one subject name, an optional exam date and weak-subject flag per subject, and daily study hours.
  • The student's time zone is detected from the browser and saved without asking.
  • The "Finish" button stays disabled until at least one subject has a name.
  • After finishing, the dashboard lists the subjects entered, and setup is not shown again while the student has at least one subject.

Not included: Entering the class timetable during setup (done later in US-007), and importing subjects from a university portal.

Scene 2

Subjects and timetable

The foundation everything else hangs on: what Priya studies, when her exams are, and when her classes run.

US-005

Manage subjects

Not reviewed
Priya's moment

Priya adds "DBMS", fixes the typo in "Cumputer Networks", and deletes an elective she dropped, along with its old assignments.

Think of it like

Subjects are the folders in your school bag. Every assignment, note and attendance mark goes inside one.

Why it's needed

Subject is the root of the data model: assignments, sessions, notes and attendance all belong to one. The synopsis has no way to create or change subjects outside the planner form, so a student who mistypes a subject or drops a course is stuck.

As a student, I want to add, rename and delete my subjects, so that my assignments, notes, plans and attendance are organised by subject.

How we'll know it works (5 checks)
  • The student can add a subject with a name of 1 to 100 characters.
  • Adding a second subject with the same name (ignoring case) is rejected with a message.
  • The student can rename a subject, and the new name appears everywhere it is used.
  • Deleting a subject shows a confirmation that states how many assignments, notes, study sessions and attendance records will also be removed.
  • After confirming, the subject and all those records are gone, and none of them appear on any screen or report.

Not included: A shared subject catalogue, semesters, and archiving subjects instead of deleting them.

Open question

Should subjects be archived at the end of a semester rather than deleted, so old attendance and notes stay available?

Related decisions: ADR-011 ADR-023

US-006

Set exam dates and weak subjects

Not reviewed
Priya's moment

Priya sets her DBMS exam for 12 December and marks Maths as a weak subject. The dashboard now says "DBMS exam in 9 days".

Think of it like

Like circling exam dates in red on the wall calendar and putting a star next to the subject you find hardest.

Why it's needed

Exam dates and weak areas are the main inputs to the planner, yet the synopsis only collects them inside the planner form. Students revise poorly when difficult subjects get the same time as easy ones.

As a student, I want to set each subject's exam date and mark subjects I find difficult, so that my study plan and dashboard focus on what is coming up and what I struggle with.

How we'll know it works (4 checks)
  • The student can set, change and clear an exam date for each subject.
  • An exam date before today is rejected with a message.
  • The student can toggle a subject's weak flag, and weak subjects show a visible "Weak" label in the subject list and planner form.
  • The dashboard's "Upcoming exams" section lists subjects with an exam in the next 30 days, soonest first, with the number of days remaining.

Not included: More than one exam per subject (mid-term and final), and exam times or venues.

Open question

Do any subjects need more than one exam date, for example a practical and a theory exam?

Related decisions: ADR-010

US-007

Enter weekly class timetable

Not reviewed
Priya's moment

Priya enters her counselling sessions: DBMS every Monday and Wednesday, 6 to 7 pm. On Monday the dashboard shows them under "Today's classes".

Think of it like

Like the class timetable pinned on the noticeboard. The app needs its own copy to know what "today" looks like.

Why it's needed

The synopsis shows "today's classes" on the dashboard but never collects the timetable, so that section would always be empty. Students currently keep timetables in a separate calendar app that knows nothing about attendance.

As a student, I want to enter my weekly class timetable, so that the dashboard shows today's classes and attendance can warn me based on the classes still to come.

How we'll know it works (5 checks)
  • The student can add a class slot with a subject, weekday, start time and end time.
  • A slot whose end time is not after its start time is rejected with a message.
  • A slot overlapping another slot on the same weekday is rejected with a message naming the conflicting class.
  • The student can edit and delete slots.
  • The dashboard's "Today's classes" section lists today's slots in start-time order, using the student's time zone.

Not included: One-off changes (cancelled or extra classes, holidays), non-weekly patterns, and importing a timetable from a university portal (synopsis §10).

Open question

How should holidays and cancelled classes be handled, given that attendance warnings depend on future classes?

The daily dashboard

The one screen Priya opens every morning.

US-008

View daily dashboard

Not reviewed
Priya's moment

Each morning Priya opens one screen: two classes today, a DBMS assignment due tomorrow, Maths exam in 9 days, Networks attendance at risk, and a 5-day streak.

Think of it like

Like a car dashboard: fuel, speed and warning lights in one glance, without opening the bonnet.

Why it's needed

The core problem in the synopsis is fragmentation: deadlines in chat groups, timetables in calendar apps, attendance counted by hand. A single summary screen is the main reason a student would open CampusMate daily.

As a student, I want one dashboard that shows today's classes, pending assignments, upcoming exams, attendance and my progress, so that I can see what needs my attention without opening several apps.

How we'll know it works (5 checks)
  • The dashboard shows five sections: today's classes, pending assignments sorted by due date, upcoming exams, attendance percentage per subject, and XP with current streak.
  • Pending assignments due today or tomorrow are visually highlighted (US-027).
  • Subjects below or at risk of falling below the attendance threshold show a warning label (US-033).
  • A section with no data shows a short explanation and a link to the screen where that data is added.
  • The dashboard shows only the signed-in student's data.

Not included: Customising, reordering or hiding dashboard sections.

Related decisions: ADR-011 ADR-012

US-009

Get a daily AI study suggestion

Not reviewed
Priya's moment

Her dashboard says: "Revise normalisation in DBMS for 45 minutes. Your exam is in 9 days." It says the same thing all day, and something new tomorrow.

Think of it like

Like a tutor who leaves you one sticky note each morning instead of giving a lecture.

Why it's needed

Students with limited supervision often start a study session unsure what to prioritise. A single, specific suggestion based on their exams, deadlines and attendance turns the stored data into a next action.

As a student, I want one short AI study suggestion on my dashboard each day, so that I know what to focus on today without planning it myself.

How we'll know it works (4 checks)
  • The dashboard shows a one-sentence suggestion that names at least one of the student's subjects or assignments.
  • Reloading the dashboard on the same day (in the student's time zone) shows the same suggestion and makes no new AI request.
  • The first dashboard load on the next day shows a newly generated suggestion.
  • If the AI service fails, a fixed fallback message is shown instead, no error page appears, and generation is retried on the next load.

Not included: Chatting with the AI, more than one suggestion per day, and a button to regenerate the suggestion.

Related decisions: ADR-012 ADR-018 ADR-020

Scene 4

Smart planner

Turning exams, weak subjects and spare hours into a study plan that adapts when life gets in the way.

US-010

Generate an AI study timetable

Not reviewed
Priya's moment

Priya says she has 2 hours a day. CampusMate builds a plan from today to her last exam, giving Maths extra time.

Think of it like

Like a GPS planning the route to your exam date, given how much fuel (time) you have each day.

Why it's needed

Calendar apps know nothing about exams or weak subjects, so students either plan revision by hand or not at all. Planning is where students with no day-to-day supervision struggle most, and it is the main AI feature of CampusMate.

As a student, I want an AI-generated study timetable based on my subjects, weak areas, exam dates and daily study hours, so that I have a realistic revision plan without building one by hand.

How we'll know it works (6 checks)
  • The planner form is pre-filled with the student's subjects, weak flags, exam dates and saved daily study hours.
  • Generating a plan creates study sessions with status "planned" from today up to the latest exam date.
  • No day's planned sessions add up to more than the student's daily study hours.
  • No session for a subject is scheduled after that subject's exam date.
  • Every subject with a future exam date has at least one session.
  • If the AI response breaks any of these rules or the AI is unavailable, a clear error is shown and no sessions are saved (TC-08).

Not included: Time-of-day scheduling (sessions are dated, not timed), avoiding class times, manual drag-and-drop editing, and syncing to Google Calendar.

Open question

What measurable rule defines "more time for weak subjects" (for example, at least 1.5 times the minutes of a non-weak subject with the same exam date) so it can be tested?

Related decisions: ADR-005 ADR-010 ADR-018 ADR-024

US-011

Save daily study hours

Not reviewed
Priya's moment

Priya set "2 hours a day" once. Next week, when she regenerates her plan, the form already says 2.

Think of it like

Like your phone remembering your home address so you don't type it every time you book a cab.

Why it's needed

The planner needs available hours, but the synopsis schema has no column for them, so they would be lost after each generation. Re-entering them each time is tedious and invites inconsistent plans.

As a student, I want my daily study hours to be saved, so that I don't re-enter them every time I generate or adjust a plan.

How we'll know it works (4 checks)
  • The student can set daily study hours between 0.5 and 12, in steps of 0.5.
  • Values outside that range are rejected with a message.
  • The saved value pre-fills the planner form on later visits, including after signing out and back in.
  • Changing the value does not alter the existing plan until the student regenerates it (US-015).

Not included: Different hours for different weekdays.

Open question

Do students need different hours on weekends and weekdays?

Related decisions: ADR-020

Priya opens the timetable and sees Monday to Sunday: today highlighted, finished sessions marked done, the one she missed marked missed.

US-013

Mark a study session completed

Not reviewed
Priya's moment

After 45 minutes on DBMS, Priya ticks the session done. It turns green and she earns 10 XP.

Think of it like

Ticking a box on your to-do list, and getting a gold star for it.

Why it's needed

Gamification awards XP when a session is finished, and the productivity report counts study hours, but the synopsis has no way to record completion. Without it the planner has no feedback and rewards never trigger.

As a student, I want to mark a study session as completed, so that my progress is tracked and I earn XP for studying.

How we'll know it works (4 checks)
  • A planned session dated today or earlier can be marked completed; a future-dated session cannot.
  • The session's status changes to "completed" on the timetable and in the weekly report.
  • The student is awarded session XP once; marking it again or reopening and re-completing it awards nothing more.
  • Completing a session counts as activity for today's streak.

Not included: A study timer, and recording actual minutes studied when they differ from the planned duration.

Should a session that is still "planned" automatically become "missed" once its date has passed?

US-014

Mark a session missed and reschedule

Not reviewed
Priya's moment

Her shift ran late and she missed Thursday's Maths session. She marks it missed, and the rest of the week reshuffles to fit it back in.

Think of it like

Like GPS recalculating when you miss a turn, instead of telling you to drive back to where you went wrong.

Why it's needed

Static timetables break the first time a student misses a day, and students then abandon them. Rescheduling automatically keeps the plan realistic.

As a student, I want to mark a session as missed and have my remaining plan rescheduled, so that one bad day doesn't leave my whole plan out of date.

How we'll know it works (5 checks)
  • Marking a session missed sets its status to "missed".
  • The remaining planned sessions dated today or later are replaced by a newly generated plan that meets the rules in US-010.
  • Completed and missed sessions are not changed.
  • The missed session's subject appears at least once in the new plan before its exam date.
  • If rescheduling fails, the session stays marked missed, the previous planned sessions are unchanged, and a "Try again" action is shown.

Not included: Rescheduling without the AI, and moving a single session by hand.

Related decisions: ADR-010 ADR-018 ADR-024

US-015

Regenerate the study plan

Not reviewed
Priya's moment

The DBMS exam moves from 12 to 20 December. Priya taps "Regenerate plan" and her sessions spread out over the extra week.

Think of it like

Re-planning a trip when the train date changes.

Why it's needed

Exam dates move and subjects get added mid-semester. Without regeneration the only option is to live with a plan built on old inputs.

As a student, I want to regenerate my plan after my exam dates, subjects or study hours change, so that my plan always matches my current situation.

How we'll know it works (5 checks)
  • A "Regenerate plan" action is available on the timetable screen.
  • Before regenerating, a confirmation states how many planned sessions will be replaced.
  • Only planned sessions dated today or later are replaced; completed and missed sessions stay.
  • The new plan uses the current subjects, exam dates, weak flags and daily hours.
  • If generation fails, the existing plan is left exactly as it was.

Not included: Keeping a history of previous plans, and comparing plans side by side.

Related decisions: ADR-010 ADR-018

US-016

See a clear error when AI is unavailable

Not reviewed
Priya's moment

The AI is overloaded. Priya sees "The AI service is unavailable, try again later", and her existing plan is untouched.

Think of it like

Like an ATM that shows "Out of service" instead of swallowing your card and giving you no cash.

Why it's needed

Free AI models are rate-limited and can disappear without notice (synopsis §10). Without deliberate handling, students would see spinners that never end or half-saved plans and quizzes.

As a student, I want a clear message when an AI feature can't run, so that I know what happened and that none of my data was damaged.

How we'll know it works (4 checks)
  • When an AI request fails or times out, the student sees a message saying the AI service is unavailable and to try again later, within 70 seconds of starting the action.
  • No study sessions, summaries, quizzes or suggestions are partially saved after a failure.
  • The failed action can be retried from the same screen.
  • When the student has reached the daily AI request limit, a different message says so and states when the limit resets.

Not included: Queueing failed requests to run later, and offline AI.

Related decisions: ADR-018 ADR-024

Scene 5

AI notes and quizzes

Turning a pile of PDFs into summaries and self-tests.

US-017

Upload a note and get an AI summary

Not reviewed
Priya's moment

Priya uploads a 20-page DBMS PDF and gets a one-page summary of the key points a moment later.

Think of it like

Like a friend who reads the whole chapter and gives you the key points on one page.

Why it's needed

Students keep notes as scattered PDFs and photos with no summaries, so revision means rereading everything. Summaries generated from the student's own material are the second main AI feature.

As a student, I want to upload my notes for a subject and get an AI summary, so that I can revise the key points quickly.

How we'll know it works (5 checks)
  • The upload screen requires choosing a subject and a file.
  • The upload screen shows a one-line notice that notes are processed by an external AI service.
  • PDF and plain-text files up to 10 MB are accepted.
  • After a successful upload the AI summary is shown, and reopening the note later shows the same summary without a new AI request.
  • A PDF with no extractable text is rejected with a message explaining it may be a scanned document.

Not included: Images and handwritten notes (unless ADR-015 is amended), Word and PowerPoint files, and editing the summary.

Open question

Is 10 MB the right size limit? Do students need Word (.docx) or PowerPoint (.pptx) support?

US-018

Generate a quiz from a note

Not reviewed
Priya's moment

From the same PDF, CampusMate writes 8 multiple-choice questions about normalisation and keys.

Think of it like

Like a friend who quizzes you from your own notes the night before an exam.

Why it's needed

Self-testing is more effective than rereading, but writing questions takes time students don't have. General chatbots can do it but aren't linked to the student's notes or progress.

As a student, I want a multiple-choice quiz generated from each note I upload, so that I can test myself on my own material.

How we'll know it works (4 checks)
  • Each successfully processed note has a quiz of 5 to 10 multiple-choice questions.
  • Every question has exactly four options and exactly one correct answer.
  • The quiz is saved with the note and shown again without a new AI request.
  • A quiz response that doesn't meet these rules is rejected and nothing is saved (US-016).

Not included: True/false, fill-in-the-blank or short-answer questions, and choosing difficulty.

Open question

Should the student choose the number of questions, or is a fixed number fine?

US-022

Regenerate a summary or quiz

Not reviewed
Priya's moment

The summary skipped the topic Priya cared about. She taps "Regenerate summary" and gets a better one; her old quiz scores stay.

Think of it like

Asking a friend to explain it again, a different way.

Why it's needed

US-023

Reject invalid uploads

Not reviewed
Priya's moment

Priya tries to upload a Word file. CampusMate tells her straight away it accepts PDF and text files only.

Think of it like

Like a vending machine that hands back a foreign coin and shows which coins it takes.

Why it's needed

Unsupported or oversized files would otherwise fail deep inside text extraction or the AI call, wasting the student's time and the AI quota.

As a student, I want a clear message when I upload a file CampusMate can't use, so that I know what to upload instead.

How we'll know it works (4 checks)
  • Uploading an unsupported type (for example .exe or .docx) is rejected with a message listing the allowed types (TC-07).
  • Uploading a file over the size limit is rejected with a message stating the limit.
  • Both checks are also enforced by the API: a direct request with an invalid file returns HTTP 400 or 422.
  • No note is created and no AI request is made for a rejected file.

Not included: Virus scanning.

Scene 6

Each decision (an ADR, Architecture Decision Record) is a technical choice and what it costs. "In the synopsis" means the synopsis already makes this choice; "Needs your decision" means it's new or changes the synopsis and should be agreed with your Project Guide.

Foundation

The big structural choices: how the app is split up and what it is built with.

ADR-001

Three-tier web architecture

In the synopsisNot reviewed
In plain words

CampusMate is split into three layers: the screens students see (Angular), the brain that applies the rules (FastAPI), and the memory that stores everything (the database).

Think of it like

A restaurant: the dining room (screens), the kitchen (rules), the storeroom (database). Customers never walk into the kitchen, so the secret recipe (the AI key) stays safe.

If you approve

You learn two languages instead of one, and in return the AI key stays safe and the code matches the diagrams in your synopsis.

You gain
  • The OpenRouter key and all business rules stay on the server.
  • Each tier maps directly onto the synopsis's architecture diagram and DFD processes.
You accept
  • Two languages (TypeScript and Python) to learn in parallel.
  • Cross-origin requests need CORS configuration and token passing on every call.
Full reasoning

Why decide now: CampusMate needs a browser UI, business logic (planner rules, attendance maths, gamification), relational storage and calls to two external services (Firebase, OpenRouter). The OpenRouter key must never reach the browser. Without a fixed structure, logic would drift between client and server and the DFDs in the synopsis would stop matching the code.

Chosen: Three-tier: Angular + FastAPI + relational DB. Also considered:

  • Django full-stack with server-rendered pages
  • Firebase-only backend-as-a-service, logic in the browser

Why not the others: A Firebase-only design is rejected because the project is registered under the RDBMS category and needs a relational schema. Django is rejected because the synopsis commits to Angular and FastAPI.

When to rethink: Reopen if the Angular learning phase overruns by more than one week, or if the Project Guide asks for a simpler single-codebase design.

How we keep to it: PR review checklist: no database access or OpenRouter calls in frontend/. A CI step fails if the string "openrouter" appears anywhere under frontend/.

  • Reports always match the live data.
  • Aggregate queries demonstrate SQL skills for the report.
You accept
  • Past weeks can't be compared unless re-computed.
  • Printed output is only as good as the browser's print styling.
Full reasoning

Why decide now: Section 5.5 lists four reports but no module, storage or export format. Precomputed reports can go stale; generating PDFs on the server adds libraries.

Chosen: On-request SQL aggregates, CSV and browser print. Also considered:

  • Precomputed snapshot tables
  • Server-side PDF generation (ReportLab or WeasyPrint)

Why not the others: Snapshot tables are rejected because they need a scheduler. Server-side PDFs are rejected as extra work for little gain.

When to rethink: Reopen if the Guide requires formatted PDF reports, or if report queries exceed one second.

How we keep to it: Tests compare each report's figures with a fixed fixture data set.
